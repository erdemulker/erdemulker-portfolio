Case 06

Group travel app, mobile

One plan for where the group eats and who pays

A mobile app for group trips, designed around one evening in Lisbon. Three friends decide where to eat, the plan updates itself, and they settle up with the fewest transfers and no arithmetic.

3 actsdecide, pay and settle, played end to end
1 objectthe poll result, the plan item and the expense
2 phonesthe same moment from the person owed and the person paying

It has not launched yet, so there are no usage numbers. What the design proves is the link: every screen reuses a decision or a cost that already exists in the plan, so nobody types anything twice.

RoleProduct design and the working prototype
TeamSolo
Year2026
The decision that mattered

Four options at most, closed automatically once everyone has voted. On a tie, the creator’s pick wins.

What it cost

Less democratic, and there is no runoff. In return nobody has to be the one who calls it, which is where group chats stall.

The plan after the vote. Dinner came from the poll, and the score stays on the item.

Context

Group trips break down at two points. The decision happens in a chat thread, where the loudest voice wins and nobody commits. The money happens later, in a splitting app, as a pile of small transfers and reminders. The plan sits in a third place, and nothing connects them.

Before

Three tools for one dinner: a chat to argue in, a notes app for the plan, a splitting app for the bill.

Tension

A map of the whole app would have been the safe deliverable, and shallow everywhere. I scoped it to one evening and played it end to end with real states, so every rule had to survive being used.

Insight

Plan, decision and expense are one object. A poll result should become a plan item, and an expense should attach to the item it paid for. Once that holds, the app does the bookkeeping people now do in their heads.

The poll result is the plan item. The bill is attached to it.

Decisions, and what each one cost

An open poll, or one that closes itself
What I choseFour options at most, closed automatically once everyone has voted. On a tie, the creator’s pick wins.
What it costLess democratic, and there is no runoff. In return nobody has to be the one who calls it, which is where group chats stall.
Report the result, or act on it
What I choseThe winner replaces the empty evening slot in everyone’s plan, with the score on the item.
What it costChanging the plan after a result needs its own path. I left it as a stub.
Split the bill, or split the items
What I chosePer item. Each line has its own people, so nobody pays for wine they did not drink. An equal split stays the default.
What it costMore input than one total divided by three. The default keeps the quick case quick.
Pay back each expense, or net the trip
What I choseNet everything into the fewest transfers, with a “Why only 2” note that explains the maths. The person paying sees an itemised list of what for.
What it costIndividual debts drop out of the main view. The explainer and the itemised list are there to earn that trust back.

The three acts

The empty evening slot starts the poll. Up to four places, picked from the map or typed in.

The poll closes on the last vote. The winner is in everyone’s plan before anyone asks.

The expense is already linked to dinner. Balances count from their old value, so the change reads before the total.

Two transfers for the whole trip. Then the other phone, where the request says exactly what it covers.

More: craft details

Craft

It is a working app in plain JavaScript, not a click-through file, so timing and state could be judged on a real phone.

  • Balances count upEach balance animates from its old value to its new one in 900ms.
  • HapticsA short vibration on votes, payments and selections.
  • NotificationsEvery banner opens the screen it talks about. They are what make it feel shared.
  • State that survivesThe demo keeps its state, so a reload on a phone does not lose the evening.

Looking back

The first thing I would test with real groups is whether auto-close with a creator tie-break feels fair to the person who lost. Onboarding, trip creation and more than one trip are stubs on purpose. Next it needs more than one currency, partial payments, and the friend who leaves before anyone settles.
